# New visual work

Use this flow when creating a new surface or deliberately replacing an existing visual world. Do not use it for a local refinement whose identity should remain stable.

## 1. Decide what is already true

Inspect the user's requirements, the active design system, tokens, shared components, assets, representative screens, and neighboring flows.

Classify the work:

- **Extension:** a coherent world exists. Inherit it and solve only the new purpose, content, hierarchy, states, interaction, and connection to its surroundings.
- **New world:** no coherent identity exists. Establish one from product truth, audience, context, and supplied evidence.
- **Replacement world:** the user rejected or superseded the current direction. Preserve product truth and protected behavior, but do not dilute the replacement with the abandoned style.

A missing design document does not mean a missing identity. Rendered screens and code can still be authoritative evidence.

## 2. Resolve only consequential uncertainty

Determine:

- audience, context, and visitor mode;
- the primary job, action, or understanding;
- required content, proof, controls, and states;
- platform, input method, responsive range, and fidelity;
- protected product facts, behavior, brand rules, and assets;
- whether the user wants refinement, exploration, or committed execution.

Ask only when an unresolved answer would materially change the artifact. State reversible assumptions and continue.

## 3. Identify the product-specific opportunity

Before choosing a style, name:

- the product mechanism or content that deserves to become the visual anchor;
- the audience's real emotional and operational context;
- the category-standard arrangement the work should either use deliberately or refuse;
- the one moment users should remember;
- the strongest existing evidence that the direction must respect.

Avoid directions described only by adjectives. “Clean, modern, premium” is not a direction until it determines structure, typography, color, material, behavior, and content hierarchy.

## 4. Explore materially different worlds

When exploration is appropriate, consider two or three coherent directions that differ in form, not merely decoration. Change at least several of:

- information topology and first-viewport composition;
- type character and role contrast;
- palette strategy and color dosage;
- density, spacing rhythm, shape, and elevation;
- imagery, data, illustration, or product-artifact treatment;
- navigation and interaction grammar;
- signature product-specific module or motion moment.

Fuse each direction with the real product. A reference contributes system discipline, not its literal clothes. Reject any direction that cannot carry the product's truth or primary task.

Compare directions on audience identification and product clarity. Familiar and effective is a legitimate choice when selected deliberately; novelty is not a quality metric.

## 5. Commit the chosen world

Record a concise direction contract:

- **Thesis:** the single idea the surface owns and the default treatment it refuses.
- **World:** palette roles, type character, component language, material, and imagery.
- **Story or task:** what the visitor understands, believes, or completes.
- **First viewport:** exact hierarchy, composition, scale, and primary action location.
- **Signature:** the product-specific module or interaction that makes the work recognizable.
- **Responsive behavior:** what reorders, collapses, persists, or disappears.
- **Risk:** the honest usability, implementation, content, or familiarity risk.

If any item still reads like a mood, the direction is not decided.

## 6. Build with full commitment

Render one valid representative surface early. Apply the chosen grammar to every visible atom: navigation, controls, fields, states, copy hierarchy, imagery, data, and motion. A stock component left inside a highly authored world should still be intentionally integrated.

Use real product structure and honest sample content. Never invent commercial claims, customer evidence, or metrics merely to complete the composition.

For Design sessions, show coherent artboards, flows, states, and meaningful variations inside the existing canvas. For App sessions, keep the running product usable and make states functional rather than illustrative.

## 7. Inspect and finish

Compare the rendered result against the direction contract, not against how much code was written. Inspect representative desktop and mobile evidence together.

Verify:

- the product-specific idea survives without explanatory prose;
- hierarchy makes the primary task or promise immediate;
- all screens and states belong to one world;
- responsive changes preserve priority rather than merely shrinking;
- interaction, accessibility, and content extremes remain credible;
- the result is neither a timid compromise nor spectacle detached from the job.
